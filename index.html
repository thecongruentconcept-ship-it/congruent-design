The Congruent Method

Every project follows the same five steps, from the first site visit to the years after handover.

  1. Assess

    The site as it is: sun, soil, slope, water, views and how it will be used.

  2. Align

    Scope, budget and priorities agreed before design begins.

  3. Design

    One complete plan: planting, hardscape, grading, lighting and irrigation.

  4. Execute

    Built by licensed contractors, with Congruent confirming the work matches the plan.

  5. Steward

    After handover, we assess the landscape and direct the care it needs.

A planning estimate, not a prescription. How far water spreads under an emitter varies from yard to yard, so Congruent confirms every schedule with a soil probe on site. For established plants on pressure-compensating drip, 90% efficient. Each 1 gallon-per-hour emitter wets a circle of soil about 1.5 feet across in sand and 3 feet across in sandy loam and loam (USDA NRCS National Engineering Handbook 623, Ch. 7, Table 7-14), and only wetted soil stores water for the plant. Soil water-holding: Lake Havasu soil survey values (USDA NRCS SSURGO, Mohave County AZ627: about 0.05 in/in in wash sands, 0.11 in/in in the common gravelly loams) and a typical loam (about 0.15 in/in; USDA texture tables). Water is applied when half of that stored water is used. Weather: typical daily reference ET for the Lower Colorado River desert (CIMIS Zone 18); live readings, when available, come from the AZMET Fort Mohave station. Plant water factors: ANSI/ASABE S623.1, with each plant classed by its WUCOLS V Low Desert rating where one exists (WUCOLS V, California Center for Urban Horticulture, University of California, Davis, 2025). AZMET data are used as published by the University of Arizona; AZMET does not endorse this calculator. New plantings need more frequent water until established.
